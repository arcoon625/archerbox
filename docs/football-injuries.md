# Touchdown Rush injury and substitution notes

## Game studied

Studied the official NFL gamebook and play-by-play for the **2024 AFC Championship, Buffalo at Kansas City, played January 26, 2025 (Bills 29, Chiefs 32)**. This was a review of the written game record, not a viewing of the full broadcast.

- [Official NFL gamebook](https://static.www.nfl.com/image/upload/v1737977399/gamecenter/12bd1cfd-d5fc-11ef-929f-5655038981b2.pdf)
- [Bills' official Christian Benford injury update](https://www.buffalobills.com/news/bills-issued-this-injury-update-on-cb-christian-benford)
- [NFL return-to-participation protocol](https://www.nfl.com/playerhealthandsafety/resources/fact-sheets/nfl-return-to-participation-protocol)

Observed examples in the gamebook:

| Player | What the written record showed | Useful game behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Justin Reid | Injured in Q1 after a James Cook run; returned a few plays later | Tacklers can leave briefly |
| Nikko Remigio | Injured on a Q1 kickoff return; returned during Q2 | Return personnel also need substitutes |
| Christian Benford | Injured in Q1 after tackling Xavier Worthy; the official team update ruled him out with a concussion | Some absences last the rest of the game |
| Nick Bolton | Injured on Cook's Q2 touchdown; returned later that quarter | A score and an injury report are separate events |
| Kareem Hunt | Injured at Q2 3:39; returned at 2:49 | A ball carrier can miss a couple of plays |
| Chris Jones | Injured early in Q4; returned at 13:47 | Line players can also be affected |

The observed return timings only establish that short and longer absences occurred in this game. They do not establish a medical recovery timeline, a diagnosis, or any particular athlete's injury likelihood. The real concussion protocol requires clinical evaluation and clearance, not a fixed number of plays. The arcade game therefore keeps every head evaluation out for the rest of the current match with no override.

## Original arcade model

One game cannot establish a reliable NFL injury rate. These probabilities and labels are **original gameplay estimates**, informed by the situations and substitutions above, not measured NFL probabilities or predictions about real athletes.

- At most one draw at a completed, actual tackle/sack; no per-frame random checks. Timeout/administrative tackles, incompletions, standing idle and menu time cannot cause injuries.
- Estimate: 1.2% per eligible contact, plus up to 1.6 percentage points for relative movement speed. A return adds 0.15 percentage points, capped at 3%. The speed/return terms are game balancing assumptions, not a medically validated impact model.
- At least four completed plays between incidents, no repeated patient, and at most three incidents in a match. A normal three-minute game will often have none.
- Eligible subjects: ball carrier, actual tackler, or a nearby engaged blocker. A player without a valid positional reserve is skipped; the game does not invent a specialist or put the wrong player in that position.
- Fictional ankle/shoulder knocks miss three/two subsequent completed plays respectively. A leg strain or head evaluation stays out for this match. These are compressed arcade statuses; the game does not diagnose real injuries or say a real injury heals after a few plays.
- No graphic injury effects. A player briefly kneels after the whistle. The next lineup replaces the player automatically and refreshes their jersey/model. Scoring/pass banners remain separate from the sideline update.
- The status panel clearly calls these fictional injuries. They never alter the dated NFL roster source, human fantasy availability, future matches or real-world player news.
- New matches start healthy. Setup has a saved On/Off preference. Local **Try Injury Substitutions** is a labeled scripted practice example, suppresses further random incidents, and uses the actual game lineup/return code. It cannot submit friend/fantasy results.

## Reserves and shared matches

`reserves.js` is a separate dated snapshot generated by `scripts/update-injury-reserves.py` from ESPN's team depth-chart and roster APIs. The October 19, 2026 snapshot has 1,801 eligible identities across all 32 clubs. Only roster offense/defense/special-team groups and active/day-to-day statuses are accepted. IR/out, practice-squad and suspended groups are excluded. This is a roster snapshot, not an assertion of a current game-day active list.

Depth-chart order selects a reserve before the remaining eligible players in the position family. Current starters, on-field teammates, listed specialists, already assigned substitutes and unavailable players are excluded. All original/custom jersey numbers are reserved to prevent later unit conflicts. Backups use their own recorded jersey number. For example, Dallas uses Sam Howell #16 behind Dak Prescott and Tyler Goodson #32 behind Javonte Williams in this snapshot. Dallas' QB2 relationship was also checked against the [Cowboys' official report](https://www.dallascowboys.com/news/sam-howell-on-earning-qb2-job-the-work-does-n-t-stop-now).

The same availability rules apply to offense, defense, kick protection/coverage and return units. All lineups retain eleven distinct people and numbers. Injured backups can themselves be replaced; replacement chains are bounded by the match incident cap.

For friend matches the inviting player's setting applies to both sides. The server owns the serialized random state and all injury/return events. Both clients receive the same outcome. Guest views remap team indexes but retain real athlete IDs. There are no client commands to injure, heal or force a return.

## Verification

`node tests/football-injuries.test.mjs` checks all 32 teams, 700 available positional substitutions, unique eleven-player units through kicks and possession changes, real reserve identities/numbers, customized jerseys, completed-play absence counters, short returns, game-ending statuses, injury-off, idle/no-contact/practice exclusions, exact-once whistles, rare seeded contact trials, cap/cooldown, reset, serialized determinism, guest team remapping and server authority. It also checks that touchdowns and fantasy yard/TD stats continue to work.

Existing football, passing, plays, blocking, punts/returns, field goals/PATs, special teams, player models, stadium, celebrations, multiplayer, friends, fantasy and bundle checks remain regressions. Browser testing uses the actual practice button and ordinary play controls; no browser state injection is used.
